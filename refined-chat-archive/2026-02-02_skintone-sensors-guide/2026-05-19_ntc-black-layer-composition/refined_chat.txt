The black coating on an NTC thermistor like the one in your image is usually made from a **ceramic semiconductor material** covered with a **protective epoxy or glass layer**.  

### Inside Material (Core)
The sensing part is mainly made of metal oxide ceramics such as:

- **Manganese oxide (Mn₂O₃)**
- **Nickel oxide (NiO)**
- **Cobalt oxide (Co₃O₄)**
- **Copper oxide (CuO)**
- Sometimes iron oxide

These oxides are mixed and sintered at high temperature to form the NTC bead.

### Black Outer Layer
The black bead you see is generally:

- **Epoxy resin coating** (most common in cheap bead thermistors)
- or **Glass coating** (for high precision / medical / industrial grade)

The coating protects:
- moisture
- oxidation
- mechanical damage
- electrical leakage

### Structure
Typical layer structure:

1. Metal leads (silver/nickel wires)
2. Semiconductor ceramic bead
3. Conductive contact layer
4. Black epoxy/glass protective coating

### Why Black?
The black color usually comes from:
- carbon-filled epoxy
- heat-resistant resin pigments
- oxide surface coloration

### Important Notes

- Keep the silicone layer **thin**
- Thick cubes slow down response time
- More material = slower temperature change detection

- Small cube → OK
- Large thick block → slow readings

- body temperature sensors

- use thermally conductive silicone with known thermal conductivity

- called **Acetoxy RTV**

Better type:
- **Neutral cure RTV silicone**

- electronics-grade RTV
- neutral cure thermal silicone

Here’s a concise breakdown of the chemical compositions for **PDMS** and **RTV silicone**:

---

### 1. PDMS (Polydimethylsiloxane)

**Chemical Formula:**  
\[
[-Si(CH_3)_2-O-]_n
\]

**Structure:**  
- Linear siloxane polymer backbone: Si-O-Si  
- Methyl groups (CH₃) attached to silicon atoms  
- Degree of polymerization \(n\) controls viscosity and flexibility

**Typical Additives for Medical/Conductive Use:**  
- Thermal fillers: Boron Nitride (BN), Aluminum Oxide (Al₂O₃), Aluminum Nitride (AlN)  
- Curing agents (for crosslinking): Platinum catalysts for addition cure, or peroxides for condensation cure  
- Stabilizers, inhibitors, pigments (optional, often biocompatible)

**Properties:**  
- Flexible, biocompatible  
- Electrically insulating  
- Soft thermal interface  
- Stable over wide temperature range (-50°C to 200°C)

---

### 2. RTV Silicone (Room Temperature Vulcanizing Silicone)

**Chemical Base:**  
- Polysiloxane (same PDMS backbone)  
- Functional end groups: hydroxyl (-OH) or vinyl (-CH=CH₂)  
- Crosslinking occurs at room temperature via:

**Types of RTV Curing Chemistry:**  
1. **Acetoxy RTV:**  
   - Crosslinks with moisture, releasing acetic acid (CH₃COOH)  
   - Formula simplified:  
     \[
     HO-Si-(CH_3)_2-O-Si-(CH_3)_2-OH + Moisture → Crosslinked Si-O-Si network + CH_3COOH
     \]

2. **Neutral Cure RTV:**  
   - Crosslinks without releasing corrosive acid  
   - Often uses oxime or alkoxy chemistry:  
     \[
     Si-OR + H_2O → Crosslinked Si-O-Si + ROH
     \]  
   - Biocompatible for medical applications

**Fillers/Additives for Thermal Conductivity:**  
- Aluminum Oxide, Boron Nitride, Silica, Ceramic powders  
- Pigments or UV stabilizers (optional)  

**Properties:**  
- Room temperature cure  
- Flexible, soft, waterproof  
- Excellent thermal interface material  
- Safe for medical electronics (especially neutral cure)
