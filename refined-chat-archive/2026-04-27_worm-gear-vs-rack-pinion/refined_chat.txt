## **Worm Gear (not “warm” gear… unless it’s emotionally supportive machinery)**  
image_group{"aspect_ratio":"1:1","query":["worm gear and worm wheel diagram","worm gear set close up","worm drive mechanism industrial","worm gear reducer assembly"],"num_per_query":1}

A **worm gear** system has:
- A **worm** → looks like a screw  
- A **worm wheel** → looks like a normal gear  

### What it does:
- Converts **rotational motion between non-parallel shafts** (usually 90°)
- Gives **very high speed reduction**
- Can be **self-locking** (won’t rotate backward easily)

### Why engineers like it:
- Massive torque increase  
- Smooth and quiet operation  
- Great for lifts, conveyors, tuning mechanisms  

### Real-world vibe:
Think of it as a slow, powerful grinder. Not fast, but unstoppable.

## **Rack and Pinion Gear**  
image_group{"aspect_ratio":"1:1","query":["rack and pinion gear mechanism diagram","rack and pinion steering system car","linear motion rack gear teeth","rack and pinion industrial setup"],"num_per_query":1}

This one’s more straightforward.

It has:
- A **pinion** → circular gear  
- A **rack** → straight gear (a flat bar with teeth)

### What it does:
- Converts **rotary motion → linear motion** (or vice versa)

### Why engineers use it:
- Precise linear movement  
- Simple design  
- Efficient motion transfer  

### Real-world vibe:
- Car steering systems  
- CNC machines  
- Sliding gates  

Basically, spin the gear → the rack slides. Elegant. No drama.

| Feature            | Worm Gear                     | Rack & Pinion               |
|-------------------|------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Motion Type       | Rotary → Rotary (90° angle)  | Rotary ↔ Linear             |
| Speed             | Slow                         | Faster                      |
| Torque            | Very high                    | Moderate                    |
| Back Driving      | Usually not possible         | Fully possible              |
| Use Case          | Lifts, heavy machinery       | Steering, linear motion     |

So yeah, one is a torque monster that resists being pushed back, and the other is the clean, obedient translator between spinning and sliding.
