## If You Want Maximum Precision

Choose a **0.9° stepper** instead of a standard **1.8°**.

Recommended models:
- LDO 42STH 0.9°
- STEPPERONLINE 0.9°
- Leadshine 0.9°

Advantages:
- 400 full steps/revolution
- Less resonance
- Better positioning accuracy
- Excellent for optical systems, CNC, and scientific instruments

Expect slightly lower torque than an equivalent 1.8° motor.

---

## Supply Voltage

Use **24 V**, not 12 V.

Benefits:
- Higher torque at higher RPM
- Faster acceleration
- Better microstepping performance
- Less chance of missed steps

The driver limits the current to **2 A**, so using 24 V does **not** force 24 V across the windings. It improves current rise time and high-speed performance. citeturn0search1turn0search5

## Driver Recommendation

For a 2 A motor:

- **TMC2209**: Very quiet, excellent microstepping, up to ~2 A RMS with cooling.
- **TMC5160**: Higher performance and excellent torque control.
- **DM542**: Industrial-grade digital driver, ideal if you're using a 24 V supply and want maximum torque.

For the **LDO 0.9° NEMA 17**, torque depends on the exact model because LDO makes several versions.

Here are typical values:

| Model | Step Angle | Current | Holding Torque |
|--------|------------|---------|----------------|
| LDO 42STH40-1684 | 0.9° | 1.68 A | **40 N·cm (0.40 N·m)** |
| LDO 42STH48-2004 | 0.9° | 2.0 A | **52 N·cm (0.52 N·m)** |
| LDO 42STH60-2004 | 0.9° | 2.0 A | **68 N·cm (0.68 N·m)** |

### For comparison

| Motor | Holding Torque |
|--------|---------------:|
| Typical NEMA 17 (1.5 A) | 35–45 N·cm |
| **LDO 0.9° (2 A)** | **52–68 N·cm** |
| High-torque NEMA 17 (2 A) | 59–65 N·cm |
| NEMA 23 (2 A) | 190–280 N·cm |

### Which would I choose?

For your **peristaltic pump**:
- **Need ultimate precision:** LDO **0.9° 42STH60-2004** (68 N·cm)
- **Need maximum torque while staying NEMA 17:** a **1.8° 17HS19-2004S1** (~65 N·cm). It has nearly the same holding torque, but the 0.9° motor gives twice the native step resolution and generally smoother low-speed motion.

**68 N·cm (0.68 N·m)** is already a very strong torque figure for a NEMA 17. If your application needs substantially more than that, it's usually time to move up to a NEMA 23 rather than trying to push a NEMA 17 beyond its sweet spot.
