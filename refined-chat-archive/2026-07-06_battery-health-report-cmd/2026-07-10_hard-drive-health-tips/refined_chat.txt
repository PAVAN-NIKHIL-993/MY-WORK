If you mean **how to run the Extended Self-Test or Surface Test in Hard Disk Sentinel**, here's how:

### Method 1: SMART Extended Self-Test (Recommended)
1. Select the failing drive (**ST1000DM003**).
2. Click **Disk** (top menu).
3. Go to **Short Self Test** first.
4. If it passes, go to **Disk → Extended Self Test**.
5. Wait until it completes. This may take **2-4 hours** for a 1 TB HDD.

This test checks the drive's internal condition without modifying your data.

---

### Method 2: Surface Test
1. Select the drive.
2. Click **Disk → Surface Test**.
3. Choose **Read Test** (safe, does not erase data).
4. Click **Start**.

**Do NOT choose**:
- Reinitialize Disk Surface
- Write + Read Test
- Refresh Data Area

These can overwrite data.

---

### What the results mean
- 🟢 **All green blocks:** Surface is mostly readable.
- 🟡 **Yellow blocks:** Slow sectors.
- 🔴 **Red blocks:** Bad/unreadable sectors.

That actually makes sense.

A **full zero-fill (write zeros to every sector)** can cause the drive firmware to retest every sector. During that process:

- Weak sectors that can still hold data may be marked as healthy again.
- Truly bad sectors are permanently remapped to spare sectors.
- Pending sectors often disappear after they're successfully rewritten or remapped.

So Hard Disk Sentinel recalculates the health based on the updated SMART values. The increase from **21% → 24%** means the drive is now in **slightly better condition than before**, but it is **not repaired**.

### What likely happened
- ✅ Some of the **8 weak sectors** were successfully rewritten.
- ✅ Some sectors may have been remapped.
- ❌ The **128 reallocated sectors** remain permanent.
- ❌ The disk has still experienced physical media degradation.

### Can it increase further?
Yes, but only a little.

If the weak sectors disappear and no new ones appear, you might see it climb a few more percentage points. However, unless the SMART attributes are manually adjusted by the manufacturer (which consumer drives don't do), it will **never return anywhere near 100%** because the reallocated sector count is permanent.

### What to check now
Open the **S.M.A.R.T.** tab and note these attributes:

- **05** Reallocated Sector Count
- **C5** Current Pending Sector Count
- **C6** Offline Uncorrectable
- **C7** UltraDMA CRC Error Count

If you post a screenshot of the SMART table, I can tell you:
- whether the drive is still deteriorating,
- whether the increase to 24% is a good sign,
- and whether it's reasonably safe to keep using for non-critical data.

One thing stands out from your earlier screenshot: **52,146 UltraDMA CRC (communication) errors**. That is extraordinarily high and strongly suggests you should **replace the SATA data cable** and, if possible, try a different SATA port. Those errors are usually caused by the connection rather than the disk surface itself. It's a cheap fix that can prevent a separate class of problems, because apparently even a strip of copper can decide it has had enough of existence.
