You’re basically asking for the **engineering software map**, because apparently humanity decided one CAD program was insufficient. Here’s the clean breakdown.

## 1. What each term actually means

| Tool / Field | What it means | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **EDA** | Electronic Design Automation | PCBs, schematics, electronics |
| **CAD** | Computer-Aided Design | General 2D/3D mechanical design |
| **Fusion 360** | CAD/CAM platform | 3D mechanical design, assemblies, drawings, CNC |
| **SolidWorks** | Professional mechanical CAD | Mechanical parts, assemblies, manufacturing |
| **CFD** | Computational Fluid Dynamics | Air, water, pressure, flow, heat transfer |
| **ANSYS** | Engineering simulation suite | FEA, CFD, thermal, electromagnetics, etc. |

### EDA

EDA is for **electronics**, not mechanical parts.

Typical workflow:

**Schematic → PCB layout → DRC → Gerbers → PCB manufacturing**

Good free options:

- **KiCad** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **EasyEDA** ⭐⭐⭐⭐
- **LibrePCB** ⭐⭐⭐

### CAD

CAD is the broad category.

You use it to design:

- brackets
- enclosures
- gears
- shafts
- motors
- mechanisms
- assemblies
- sheet metal
- 3D-printable parts

Good free options:

| Software | Free | Best use |
|---|---:|---|
| **FreeCAD** | ✅ | Serious parametric CAD |
| **Onshape Free** | ✅ | Cloud CAD |
| **SolveSpace** | ✅ | Lightweight parametric CAD |
| **OpenSCAD** | ✅ | Programmatic CAD |
| **Blender** | ✅ | Organic/visual modeling, not traditional engineering CAD |

# 4. CFD

CFD = **Computational Fluid Dynamics**.

Instead of physically building something and discovering that your beautifully engineered object has the aerodynamic properties of a brick, you simulate it.

You can analyze:

- airflow
- water flow
- pressure
- velocity
- turbulence
- heat transfer
- cooling
- fans
- pumps
- pipes
- ducts
- radiators
- heat sinks

Example:

**Fan → air → heat sink**

CFD can show:

```text
Air inlet
   ↓
████████████
→ → → → → →
   ↓ heat
████████████
   ↓
Hot air outlet
```

# 5. ANSYS

image_group{"layout":"carousel","aspect_ratio":"16:9","query":["ANSYS Workbench interface","ANSYS Fluent CFD simulation","ANSYS Mechanical FEA simulation"] ,"num_per_query":1}

**ANSYS isn't just CFD.**

It's a huge engineering simulation ecosystem.

Major areas include:

### Mechanical / FEA

Calculate:

- stress
- strain
- deformation
- vibration
- fatigue
- buckling
- thermal stress

### Fluent

Primarily:

**CFD**

Used for:

- airflow
- liquid flow
- turbulence
- heat transfer
- combustion
- multiphase flow

### Maxwell

Electromagnetic simulation:

- motors
- transformers
- inductors
- magnetic fields
- electromagnetic forces

### Icepak

Electronics thermal management:

- PCB cooling
- heatsinks
- fans
- enclosures
- semiconductor thermal analysis

So:

**ANSYS = simulation ecosystem**

rather than simply "a CAD program."

# 11. If you're an EC engineer

I'd actually recommend learning **five**, rather than trying to install the entire engineering software zoo:

### Electronics

**KiCad**

↓

### Mechanical CAD

**Fusion 360 OR FreeCAD**

↓

### Advanced mechanical CAD

**SolidWorks**

↓

### FEA

**ANSYS Mechanical / CalculiX**

↓

### CFD

**ANSYS Fluent / OpenFOAM**

That gives you a very strong **EC + mechanical + simulation** skill stack.

And the important distinction is:

**EDA ≠ CAD ≠ CFD ≠ FEA**

They solve different engineering problems.

```text
KiCad
  │
  └── Electronics / PCB

FreeCAD / Fusion / SolidWorks
  │
  └── Mechanical geometry

        │
        ├── FEA → "Will it break?"
        │
        ├── CFD → "How will the fluid move?"
        │
        ├── Thermal → "How hot will it get?"
        │
        └── EM → "What happens to the electromagnetic field?"
```

For your kind of electronics/mechatronics projects, **KiCad + FreeCAD/Fusion + ANSYS/OpenFOAM** is a particularly strong combination.
