**Thermal epoxy resin** is a **two-part adhesive** (Resin + Hardener) loaded with **thermally conductive ceramic fillers** such as:
- Aluminum Oxide (Al₂O₃)
- Aluminum Nitride (AlN)
- Boron Nitride (BN)
- Silicon Carbide (SiC)

It transfers heat much better than ordinary epoxy while remaining electrically insulating in most formulations. Humans somehow decided glue should also be a heatsink. Occasionally, they have good ideas.

## Properties

| Property | Typical Value |
|---------|---------------:|
| Thermal Conductivity | 0.8 to 10 W/m·K (special grades up to 20 W/m·K+) |
| Electrical Conductivity | Usually Insulating |
| Dielectric Strength | High |
| Working Time | 3-60 min |
| Full Cure | 4-72 hours |
| Temperature Range | -55°C to 200°C (depends on formulation) |
| Shrinkage | Very Low |

- Bonding heatsinks to ICs
- MOSFET mounting
- LED heat dissipation
- Power transistor attachment
- Battery thermal management
- High-power resistor mounting
- IGBT modules
- Power supplies
- RF amplifiers
- Automotive ECUs

## Thermal Conductivity Comparison

| Material | Thermal Conductivity |
|---------|--------------------:|
| Air | 0.024 W/m·K |
| Normal Epoxy | 0.2 W/m·K |
| Silicone | 0.2 W/m·K |
| Thermal Epoxy | 1-10 W/m·K |
| Thermal Pad | 1-20 W/m·K |
| Aluminum | 205 W/m·K |
| Copper | 401 W/m·K |

Notice the jump from ordinary epoxy to thermal epoxy. Still, don't expect miracles. It's "better than glue," not "secret copper in a bottle."

## Types

### 1. Electrically Insulating
Most common.

- Safe for PCB components
- Used on CPUs, LEDs, MOSFETs
- Ceramic-filled

---

### 2. Electrically Conductive

Contains silver particles.

- Used for grounding
- EMI shielding
- Electrical repairs

Not suitable where accidental shorts are possible.

Most are mixed at:

Mix thoroughly for 2-5 minutes until uniform.

## Can You Make Your Own?

Yes, though commercial products are generally more consistent.

A basic DIY approach is:
- Clear epoxy resin
- Fine aluminum oxide powder (electrically insulating)
- Or boron nitride powder (better performance, higher cost)

A filler loading of roughly **30-60% by weight** is common. Too little filler gives poor thermal performance; too much makes the mixture thick and difficult to apply.

Avoid using **metal powders** like copper or aluminum if the epoxy may contact exposed electrical conductors. They can reduce electrical insulation and create reliability issues.

---

## Thermal Conductivity by Filler

| Filler | Thermal Conductivity | Electrical |
|---------|--------------------:|-----------|
| Aluminum Oxide | 20-35 W/m·K | Insulating |
| Boron Nitride | 60-400 W/m·K | Insulating |
| Aluminum Nitride | 140-180 W/m·K | Insulating |
| Silver | ~430 W/m·K | Conductive |
| Copper | ~401 W/m·K | Conductive |

Remember, these values are for the **filler material itself**. The cured epoxy will have a much lower overall thermal conductivity because the filler is dispersed within the resin.

---

## Choosing the Right One

- **For MOSFETs, regulators, LEDs, and PCBs:** electrically insulating thermal epoxy is the usual choice.
- **For bonding a heatsink permanently:** thermal epoxy works well.
- **If you may need to remove the heatsink later:** use thermal paste with screws, clips, or spring mounts instead of thermal epoxy.
- **For very high-power electronics:** look for products with at least **3-6 W/m·K** thermal conductivity.

For most hobby and industrial electronics, a quality insulating thermal epoxy in the **1-3 W/m·K** range is already a substantial improvement over ordinary epoxy while remaining safe around circuitry.
