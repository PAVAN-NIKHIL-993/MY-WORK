If your goal is **to convert a sine wave into a safe pulsating DC signal for an MCU**, a **clipper or clamper is not the right protection method by itself**.

A clipper only limits voltage.
A clamper shifts voltage levels.

Neither guarantees safety from electric shock or MCU damage. Humans have spent decades discovering that electricity does not care about good intentions.

### First: What sine wave?

Tell me:

- Peak voltage? (5V, 12V, 24V, 120V AC, 230V AC, etc.)
- Frequency? (50 Hz, 60 Hz, kHz?)
- Is it isolated from mains?
- Do you want to:
  - Measure the waveform?
  - Detect zero crossing?
  - Detect frequency?
  - Power the MCU?

The circuit depends completely on this.

## If You Want "No Shock When Touching"

The only reliable solutions are:

1. Isolation transformer
2. Optocoupler isolation
3. Battery-powered isolated source
4. SELV voltage (<50 VAC, <120 VDC under normal conditions)

For example:

- 230VAC → Rectifier → 325V Pulsating DC

Still extremely dangerous.

If this is an **FG (Frequency Generator) output** from a DC motor and you've measured up to about **7 V AC**, then you're in a much nicer situation than mains voltage. The shock you felt is most likely due to the meter loading, floating reference, static coupling, or the FG coil generating a small current. Voltage alone does not determine shock severity; current matters too.

### What is the FG signal?

Most FG outputs are:

- Open-collector pulse output
- Hall sensor pulse output
- Small AC waveform from a dedicated FG winding

Since you're seeing AC on the meter, yours may be a **generator coil FG output**.

## Simplest MCU Interface (ESP32, 3.3 V logic)

```text
FG Signal

   47k

    |
   3.3V Zener
    |
```

Even better:

```text
FG Signal
    |
   100k
    |
    +---- GPIO
    |
   Schottky to 3.3V
    |
   Schottky to GND
```

This limits the input safely.

## Preferred Method: Convert Sine Wave to Logic Pulses

Use a comparator.

### LM393

```text
FG Signal
    |
   10k
    |
LM393 +
LM393 - = 1.65V reference

Output --> ESP32 GPIO
```

The LM393 converts:

```text
Sine:
      /\
     /  \
----/----\----

To:

____----____----
```

The ESP32 counts pulses directly.

This is how industrial motor controllers often handle tachometer signals. Humans keep inventing clever analog circuits because apparently counting waves directly would be too easy.

## Estimating Frequency at 3000 RPM

Frequency depends on pulses per revolution:

\[
f=\frac{\text{RPM} \times \text{PPR}}{60}
\]

genui{"math_block_widget_always_prefetch_v2":{"content":"f=\\frac{RPM\\times PPR}{60}"}}

Examples:

- 1 pulse/rev → 50 Hz
- 2 pulses/rev → 100 Hz
- 4 pulses/rev → 200 Hz
- 6 pulses/rev → 300 Hz

Many FG outputs are 2, 4, or 6 pulses/revolution.

---

## Before Designing Anything

Measure these with an oscilloscope if possible:

1. Peak-to-peak voltage (Vpp)
2. Frequency at known RPM
3. Whether the FG signal is:
   - Sine wave
   - Square wave
   - Open collector pulses

A photo of the motor label and the FG wiring (number of wires and colors) would help identify exactly what type of FG output it is. That saves a lot of reverse-engineering, which is humanity's favorite hobby after creating undocumented connectors.
