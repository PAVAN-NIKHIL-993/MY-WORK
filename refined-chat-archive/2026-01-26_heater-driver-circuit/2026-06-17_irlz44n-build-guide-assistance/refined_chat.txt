### Suggestions & Next Steps

- Power the ESP32 from a clean 3.3 V rail: ≥100 µF bulk + 100 nF ceramic at the VDD pins, and a separate regulator for servos/motors — brownouts masquerade as random firmware bugs.
- Respect boot strapping pins (GPIO 0/2/12 on classic ESP32, GPIO 2/8/9 on C3): no external pulls that fight the straps, and never drive GPIO12 high at reset on 5 V-tolerant modules.
- Enable the hardware watchdog (esp_task_wdt) around your main loop and use deep-sleep with RTC retention for battery nodes.
- Finish with a full ERC + DRC pass (unconnected pins, clearance, annular ring, silk over pads), define RF/antenna keepouts, and verify every footprint against its datasheet before sending to fab.
- Export Gerbers + drill files and inspect them in a viewer (gerbv/KiCad viewer) — visual inspection catches 90% of fab-house rejections.
- Drive logic-level MOSFETs directly (≤3.3 V Vgs is marginal — prefer AO3400/IRLB3034 class parts), always add a 10 kΩ gate pull-down, and flyback-protect inductive loads with a diode.
- For sustained >5 A, compute Rds(on) × I²R losses and heatsink accordingly — 7 A through 0.02 Ω is ~1 W, at the edge of TO-220 free-air ratings.

I reviewed the guide as if I were about to build it myself. There are some good parts, but there are also a few significant technical problems hiding in it. Electronics has a charming habit of working perfectly in documents and then becoming a space heater on the bench.

## 1. IRLZ44N Drive Circuit Review

### ✅ Good

- 100 Ω gate resistor is reasonable.
- 10 kΩ gate pulldown is correct.
- Low-side MOSFET arrangement is correct.
- Common ground requirement is correct.
- External supply for the coil instead of ESP32 5V pin is correct. fileciteturn0file0

---

### ⚠️ Questionable

The guide repeatedly states:

> "IRLZ44N fully ON at 3.3V"

That is an oversimplification.

IRLZ44N is a logic-level MOSFET and works much better than IRFZ44N at low gate voltages. However, "fully on" depends on current. The datasheet characterizes it at higher gate voltages, and 3.3V operation is application dependent. For a few amps it often works fine, but you cannot universally claim "fully ON at 3.3V". citeturn0search32turn0search2turn0search0

For a 5-12 W experiment it will probably work.

---

### ❌ Biggest Problem

Driving an IRLZ44N directly at **1 MHz** is not ideal.

IRLZ44N was designed as a power MOSFET, not a high-frequency RF switch.

At 1 MHz:

- Gate capacitance becomes important.
- ESP32 GPIO must charge/discharge the gate 1 million times per second.
- Switching losses become dominant.
- MOSFET heating can become much higher than predicted.

The guide suggests:

ESP32 GPIO → 100 Ω → IRLZ44N Gate

This may produce a waveform, but it is not an efficient 1 MHz power driver.

A proper RF design would use:

- MOSFET gate driver IC
- Smaller RF MOSFET
- Class-E/Class-D topology

instead. citeturn0search32turn0search4

---

## 2. ESP32 PWM Section

### ❌ Major Error

The guide configures:

```cpp
PWM_FREQ = 1000000;
PWM_RES  = 8;
```

This combination is likely impossible or severely limited.

For ESP32 LEDC:

80 MHz clock ÷ 2^resolution

For 8-bit:

80 MHz ÷ 256 ≈ 312.5 kHz

So 1 MHz with true 8-bit resolution exceeds normal LEDC limits. ESP32 reduces available resolution as frequency increases. citeturn0search9turn0search33turn0search4

The code may:

- fail,
- reduce resolution,
- not generate what is expected.

### Better

At 1 MHz:

- 5-bit or 6-bit resolution is realistic.
- 50% duty square wave is often sufficient anyway. citeturn0search4turn0search8

So the code section should be re-verified on actual hardware.
