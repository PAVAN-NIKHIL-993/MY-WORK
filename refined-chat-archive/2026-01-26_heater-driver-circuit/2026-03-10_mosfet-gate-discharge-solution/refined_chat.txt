So you poked **12 V on the gate of your IRFZ44N**, removed it, and the MOSFET keeps conducting like it’s emotionally attached to that voltage. Not magic. Just **gate capacitance** doing its lazy job.

A MOSFET gate is basically a **tiny capacitor**. When you charge it with 12 V, the charge sits there with nowhere to go. Result: the MOSFET stays ON for a while like someone refusing to leave a party.

You fix it by giving that charge an **escape route**.

---

## The Simple Fix: Gate Pull-Down Resistor

### Components
- **IRFZ44N**
- **10k resistor (Gate → Source)**

### Connection
1. **Source → GND**
2. **Drain → Load → +12 V**
3. **Gate → Control Voltage**
4. **10k resistor between Gate and Source**

### What this does
When your gate input goes to **0 V**, the resistor **drains the stored charge from the gate capacitor**, forcing the MOSFET OFF quickly.

---

## Recommended Values
| Resistor | Turn-off Speed | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 100k | Slow | Bare minimum |
| **10k** | Good | Standard value |
| 4.7k | Faster | Better for switching |
| 1k | Very fast | But wastes more current |

For most circuits:  
**10kΩ gate-to-source is perfect.**

---

## Extra Professional Touch (Optional)
Add a **series gate resistor**:

```
Control signal ──100Ω── Gate
                     |
                    10k
                     |
Source ──────────────┘
```

- **100 Ω** → protects driver and reduces ringing  
- **10 kΩ** → pulls gate down

---

## Why this problem happened
IRFZ44N gate capacitance ≈ **1–2 nF**.  
Without a resistor, that charge leaks away extremely slowly.

Your MOSFET basically said:  
“Voltage is gone? I’ll just keep this charge for a while.”

Electronics is full of parts with commitment issues.

---

## One more engineering reality check
**IRFZ44N is not a logic-level MOSFET.**

- Fully ON at **10–12 V gate**
- Weak at **3.3 V or 5 V**

If you're driving from **ESP32 or Arduino**, better choices:

- **IRLZ44N**
- **IRLB8721**
- **AO3400**

Otherwise you’ll get heat instead of switching.

No.
