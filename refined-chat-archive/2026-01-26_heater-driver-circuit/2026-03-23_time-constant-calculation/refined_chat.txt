WHAT HAPPENS IN A BASIC RC CIRCUIT?

When the capacitor charges:

When the capacitor discharges:
- Capacitor voltage decreases gradually.
- LED brightness decreases gradually.

WHY DIRECT RC CONTROL IS NOT IDEAL:

An LED is not a digital switching device.

At 3V:
- A red LED typically has a forward voltage around 1.8–2.2V.
- A blue or white LED generally requires a higher forward voltage.
- A current-limiting resistor is mandatory.
- The available voltage headroom is limited.

- BC547
- 2N2222

6. IMPORTANT TECHNICAL CLARIFICATION
==================================================

An RC time constant is NOT automatically the same as the time required to switch an LED.

For a charging capacitor:

Vc(t) = Vs × (1 - e^(-t/RC))

At one time constant:

Vc = 63.2% of the supply voltage.

For a 3V supply:

Vc = 3 × 0.632

Vc ≈ 1.90V

Therefore, with a 3V supply and a 183-second time constant, the capacitor reaches approximately 1.90V after 183 seconds.

For a discharging capacitor:

Vc(t) = Vinitial × e^(-t/RC)

After one time constant, approximately 36.8% of the initial voltage remains.

Consequently, the actual LED switching time depends on the switching threshold and circuit configuration.

A 183-second time constant does not automatically guarantee that an LED switches after exactly 183 seconds.
