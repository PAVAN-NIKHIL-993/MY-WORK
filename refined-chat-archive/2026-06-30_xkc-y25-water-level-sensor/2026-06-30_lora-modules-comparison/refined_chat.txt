## Why 433 MHz Wins

Lower frequencies have longer wavelengths.

| Frequency | Wavelength |
|-----------|------------|
| 2.4 GHz | 12.5 cm |
| 915 MHz | 33 cm |
| 433 MHz | 69 cm |

The longer wavelength:
- penetrates concrete better,
- diffracts around obstacles more effectively,
- is less affected by rebar and walls.

Physics is annoyingly consistent that way.

## Can a PCB antenna work at 433 MHz?

**Yes.**

Commercial products use:
- Meandered PCB antennas
- Inverted-F (IFA)
- Folded monopoles
- Spiral/meander traces

These electrically behave like a 17 cm antenna while physically occupying only about **40-70 mm** of PCB length.

For example:

```
#########################
#                       #
#  /\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\   #
# /\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/   #
#                       #
#########################
```

The RF current follows the long meandering path, making the antenna electrically longer than it appears.

---

## The downside

A PCB antenna is highly sensitive to:

- PCB dimensions
- Ground plane size
- Nearby batteries
- ESP32 module placement
- Plastic enclosure
- Metal screws
- User's hand
- Wiring routed near the antenna

Move a battery 10 mm closer, and the antenna can detune. RF engineers then spend days with a network analyzer muttering at invisible electromagnetic fields. The fields, naturally, refuse to apologize.
