### Suggestions & Next Steps

- Power the ESP32 from a clean 3.3 V rail: ≥100 µF bulk + 100 nF ceramic at the VDD pins, and a separate regulator for servos/motors — brownouts masquerade as random firmware bugs.
- Respect boot strapping pins (GPIO 0/2/12 on classic ESP32, GPIO 2/8/9 on C3): no external pulls that fight the straps, and never drive GPIO12 high at reset on 5 V-tolerant modules.
- Enable the hardware watchdog (esp_task_wdt) around your main loop and use deep-sleep with RTC retention for battery nodes.
- Finish with a full ERC + DRC pass (unconnected pins, clearance, annular ring, silk over pads), define RF/antenna keepouts, and verify every footprint against its datasheet before sending to fab.
- Export Gerbers + drill files and inspect them in a viewer (gerbv/KiCad viewer) — visual inspection catches 90% of fab-house rejections.
- Drive logic-level MOSFETs directly (≤3.3 V Vgs is marginal — prefer AO3400/IRLB3034 class parts), always add a 10 kΩ gate pull-down, and flyback-protect inductive loads with a diode.
- For sustained >5 A, compute Rds(on) × I²R losses and heatsink accordingly — 7 A through 0.02 Ω is ~1 W, at the edge of TO-220 free-air ratings.
- Lock in the winning decision from this chat into your project docs/repo before the next ChatGPT session; context window loss is the main failure mode.

# Arduino Code (ESP32)

```cpp
#include <Wire.h>
#include <LiquidCrystal_I2C.h>

LiquidCrystal_I2C lcd(0x27,16,2);

#define SIGNAL_PIN 34

volatile unsigned long pulseCount=0;

hw_timer_t *timer=NULL;

void IRAM_ATTR onPulse()
{
  pulseCount++;
}

void setup()
{
  Serial.begin(115200);

  lcd.init();
  lcd.backlight();

  pinMode(SIGNAL_PIN,INPUT);

  attachInterrupt(digitalPinToInterrupt(SIGNAL_PIN),onPulse,RISING);

  lcd.setCursor(0,0);
  lcd.print("LCR Meter");
}

void loop()
{
  pulseCount=0;

  delay(1000);

  float freq=pulseCount;

  float C=0.0000001;

  float L=1.0/(4.0*3.14159*3.14159*freq*freq*C);

  L=L*1000000.0;

  lcd.clear();

  lcd.setCursor(0,0);
  lcd.print("Freq:");
  lcd.print(freq);
  lcd.print("Hz");

  lcd.setCursor(0,1);
  lcd.print("L:");
  lcd.print(L);
  lcd.print("uH");
}
```

# Why Accuracy Drops

## 1. ESP32 ADC Is Not Precision Grade
ESP32 ADC:
- noisy
- nonlinear
- temperature-sensitive

Especially below low voltages.

This alone affects:
- resistance
- capacitance timing

---

# 2. Breadboard Parasitics

Breadboards add:
- stray capacitance
- stray inductance
- contact resistance

Your circuit unintentionally becomes:
> “measurement system + invisible random components.”

Especially terrible for:
- small capacitors
- small inductors

---

# 3. Internal Reference Component Tolerance

If your “100nF” capacitor is actually:
- 92nF
- 108nF

then every inductance calculation shifts.

Use:
- 1% film capacitors
- metal film resistors

Not:
> “mystery resistor from old TV repair box.”

---

# 4. Oscillator Stability

Inductance measurement depends on frequency stability.

Noise causes:
- jitter
- unstable readings
- oscillation drift

LM358 works, but it’s not exactly luxury instrumentation. It’s the engineering equivalent of:
> “I survived another day.”
