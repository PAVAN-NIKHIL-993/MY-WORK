## What probably happened

Your photo shows a **603 Junior digital multimeter** with three probe sockets:

- **COM:** black probe.
- **VΩmA:** red probe for voltage, resistance, continuity and low-current measurements.
- **10A:** red probe for high-current measurements.

You said you connected the COM and VΩmA probes across a battery while the selector was set to **10A**, even though the red probe remained in the normal VΩmA socket.

That combination can cause trouble, depending on the meter's internal circuit and the battery voltage.

When measuring a battery, the probes are connected in parallel across its terminals. In current mode, the meter is designed to have very low resistance. If the selector connects the low-resistance current circuit while the probes are across the battery, a large current can flow through the meter.

**Possible damage:**
1. The internal 200 mA fuse may have blown.
2. The PCB's current-measurement track, shunt resistor or selector contacts may have been damaged.
3. The battery may have caused damage to the voltage-input circuit, depending on the meter's design.
4. The battery itself may have been shorted or stressed.

The selector's 10A position does not make the normal VΩmA socket safe for current measurements. The probe socket matters just as much as the dial setting.
