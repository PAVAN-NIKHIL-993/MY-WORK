### What’s actually going on
An **LM358** is an op-amp. It was born to amplify slow, boring analog signals, not wrestle radio waves out of the air. RF laughs at it. Still, with some clever compromises, you *can* coerce it into acting like a **very crude RF receiver front-end**.

### What you *can* build with LM358
Think **AM detector**, not “wireless module killer.”

**Basic blocks:**
1. **Antenna**  
   A random wire. Length guesses. Ritual optimism.

2. **Diode detector (envelope detector)**  
   Converts RF into a wobbly voltage the LM358 can actually see.

3. **LM358 amplifier**  
   Boosts the detected envelope (audio or data-ish signal).

4. **Low-pass filter**  
   Strips away RF leftovers so the op-amp doesn’t panic.

This works best for:
- AM signals
- ASK / OOK (on-off keying)
- Very short range
- Very low data rates

### What it absolutely cannot do
- FM demodulation  
- High-frequency RF amplification  
- Superheterodyne magic  
- Anything above “toy-grade” reliability  

The **gain-bandwidth product** of LM358 is about **1 MHz**. RF lives in the tens to hundreds of MHz. That mismatch is not small. That’s existential.

### Realistic performance
| Feature | Reality |
|------|-------|
| Range | A few centimeters to maybe a meter |
| Frequency stability | vibes-based |
| Noise immunity | fragile |
| Repeatability | questionable |
| Debug time | eternal |

### When this makes sense
- Learning how RF detection works  
- Academic experiments  
- You want to *understand* why RF front-ends exist  
- You enjoy oscilloscopes more than success  

### When it doesn’t
- Remote controls  
- Telemetry  
- IoT  
- Anything you want to work twice in a row  

### Blunt but loving advice
If your goal is **learning analog signal flow**, go ahead. The LM358 will teach you humility and filtering.

If your goal is **a usable RF receiver**, skip the suffering and use:
- A super-regenerative RF module
- A 433 MHz ASK receiver module
- A proper RF IC
