## Why resistor values are 10, 22, 47, 68, 220, 470, etc.?

Resistors are made in **preferred value series** called the **E-series**.

The idea is:

- If a resistor has ±10% tolerance, there is no point manufacturing every possible value.
- Values are spaced so their tolerance ranges overlap nicely.
- This minimizes the number of different parts manufacturers need to make.

## Why these weird numbers?

Because they're approximately logarithmically spaced.

For E6:

\[
10^{1/6} \approx 1.47
\]

Each value is about **1.47×** the previous one.

Example:

- 10 × 1.47 ≈ 15
- 15 × 1.47 ≈ 22
- 22 × 1.47 ≈ 33
- 33 × 1.47 ≈ 47
- 47 × 1.47 ≈ 68

Humans ended up with these "odd" numbers because math said so. Humanity's favorite pastime: inventing a neat system and then forcing everyone to memorize 4.7 kΩ instead of 5 kΩ.

# 1. Why Transmission Lines Need Impedance Matching

Imagine shouting into a tunnel.

If the tunnel suddenly changes size, part of the sound comes back.

Electric signals do exactly the same thing.

When a signal travels through a cable, it sees a characteristic impedance.

Common values:

- 50 Ω
- 75 Ω
- 100 Ω
- 600 Ω

If source, cable, and load all match:

```text
Source = 50Ω
Cable  = 50Ω
Load   = 50Ω
```

signal travels cleanly.

---

## What happens if they don't match?

Example:

```text
50Ω cable
connected to
1 MΩ oscilloscope
```

Part of the signal reflects back.

```text
-----> Signal
<----- Reflection
```

These reflections cause:

- Ringing
- Overshoot
- Undershoot
- Data corruption
- RF power loss

---

- \(Z_L\) = load impedance
