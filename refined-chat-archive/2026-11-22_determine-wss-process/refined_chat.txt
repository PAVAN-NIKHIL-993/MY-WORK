## 8(b) Determine whether the given random process is Wide Sense Stationary (WSS). (6 Marks)

**Given:**

A random process is defined as

\[
X(t)=A^2\cos^2(\omega_c t+\theta)
\]

Where:
- \(A\) and \(\omega_c\) are constants.
- \(\theta\) is a random variable uniformly distributed between \(-\pi\) and \(+\pi\).

We need to determine whether \(X(t)\) is **Wide Sense Stationary (WSS)**.

### Step 1: Find the mean

The mean of a random process is

\[
E[X(t)]=E[A^2\cos^2(\omega_ct+\theta)]
\]

Since \(A\) is constant,

\[
=A^2\frac{1}{2\pi}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos^2(\omega_ct+\theta)d\theta
\]

Using,

\[
\cos^2\theta=\frac{1+\cos2\theta}{2}
\]

We get,

\[
E[X(t)]=\frac{A^2}{2}
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{E[X(t)]=\frac{A^2}{2}}
\]

The mean is constant and independent of time.

### Step 2: Find the autocorrelation function

The autocorrelation function is

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=E[X(t_1)X(t_2)]
\]

Substituting,

\[
=A^4 E[\cos^2(\omega_ct_1+\theta)
\cos^2(\omega_ct_2+\theta)]
\]

Using the trigonometric identity,

\[
\cos^2 A=\frac{1+\cos2A}{2}
\]

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=\frac{A^4}{4}
E[(1+\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta))
\]

\[
(1+\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta))]
\]

Since \(\theta\) is uniformly distributed over \(-\pi\) to \(+\pi\),

\[
E[\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta)]=0
\]

\[
E[\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta)]=0
\]

Also,

\[
E[\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta)
\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta)]
\]

\[
=\frac{1}{2}\cos[2\omega_c(t_1-t_2)]
\]

Therefore,

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=\frac{A^4}{4}
\left[1+\frac{1}{2}\cos(2\omega_c(t_1-t_2))\right]
\]

Let,

\[
\tau=t_1-t_2
\]

Hence,

\[
\boxed{R_X(\tau)=\frac{A^4}{4}
+\frac{A^4}{8}\cos(2\omega_c\tau)}
\]

### Step 3: Conclusion

The conditions for Wide Sense Stationarity are:

1. Mean should be constant and independent of time.
2. Autocorrelation should depend only on the time difference \(\tau\).

Here, both conditions are satisfied.

\boxed{\therefore X(t)\text{ is WSS}}

**Hence, the given random process is Wide Sense Stationary.**

## 8(b) Determine whether the given random process is Wide Sense Stationary (WSS). (6 Marks)

**Given:**

A random process is defined as

\[
X(t)=A^2\cos^2(\omega_ct+\theta)
\]

Where,

- \(A\) and \(\omega_c\) are constants.
- \(\theta\) is a random variable uniformly distributed between \(-\pi\) and \(+\pi\).

**To prove:** Whether the given random process is Wide Sense Stationary (WSS).

### Step 1: Definition of Wide Sense Stationary (WSS)

A random process is said to be Wide Sense Stationary if it satisfies the following two conditions:

1. The mean value of the random process must be constant and independent of time.
2. The autocorrelation function must depend only on the time difference between two time instants, not on the individual time instants.

Mathematically,

\[
E[X(t)]=\text{Constant}
\]

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=R_X(t_1-t_2)
\]

### Step 2: Calculate the Mean of the Random Process

The mean of a random process is given by

\[
E[X(t)]=E[A^2\cos^2(\omega_ct+\theta)]
\]

Since \(A\) is a constant,

\[
E[X(t)]=A^2E[\cos^2(\omega_ct+\theta)]
\]

Given that \(\theta\) is uniformly distributed between \(-\pi\) and \(+\pi\), its probability density function is

\[
f_\theta(\theta)=
\begin{cases}
\frac{1}{2\pi}, & -\pi\leq\theta\leq\pi\\
0, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
\]

Therefore,

\[
E[X(t)]=\frac{A^2}{2\pi}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos^2(\omega_ct+\theta)d\theta
\]

Using the trigonometric identity,

\[
\cos^2 x=\frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}
\]

Substituting,

\[
E[X(t)]=\frac{A^2}{4\pi}
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}[1+\cos(2\omega_ct+2\theta)]d\theta
\]

Separating the terms,

\[
=\frac{A^2}{4\pi}
\left[\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}1\,d\theta+
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos(2\omega_ct+2\theta)d\theta\right]
\]

The first integral is

\[
\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}1\,d\theta=2\pi
\]

The second integral is zero because it covers one complete period.

Therefore,

\[
E[X(t)]=\frac{A^2}{4\pi}(2\pi)
\]

\[
\boxed{E[X(t)]=\frac{A^2}{2}}
\]

Hence, the mean is constant and independent of time.

### Step 3: Calculate the Autocorrelation Function

The autocorrelation function is defined as

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=E[X(t_1)X(t_2)]
\]

Given,

\[
X(t_1)=A^2\cos^2(\omega_ct_1+\theta)
\]

\[
X(t_2)=A^2\cos^2(\omega_ct_2+\theta)
\]

Substituting,

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=A^4E[
\cos^2(\omega_ct_1+\theta)
\cos^2(\omega_ct_2+\theta)]
\]

Using the identity,

\[
\cos^2 x=\frac{1+\cos 2x}{2}
\]

We get,

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=\frac{A^4}{4}E[
(1+\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta))
\]

\[
(1+\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta))]
\]

Expanding,

\[
=\frac{A^4}{4}E[
1+\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta)
\]

\[
+\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta)
\]

\[
+\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta)
\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta)]
\]

Since \(\theta\) is uniformly distributed over \(-\pi\) to \(+\pi\),

\[
E[\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta)]=0
\]

Similarly,

\[
E[\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta)]=0
\]

Now, using the product-to-sum identity,

\[
\cos A\cos B=\frac{1}{2}
[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)]
\]

Therefore,

\[
E[\cos(2\omega_ct_1+2\theta)
\cos(2\omega_ct_2+2\theta)]
\]

\[
=\frac{1}{2}E[
\cos(2\omega_c(t_1-t_2))
\]

\[
+\cos(2\omega_c(t_1+t_2)+4\theta)]
\]

The second cosine term has zero expectation because \(\theta\) is uniformly distributed.

Hence,

\[
=\frac{1}{2}\cos(2\omega_c(t_1-t_2))
\]

Substituting into the autocorrelation equation,

\[
R_X(t_1,t_2)=\frac{A^4}{4}
\left[1+\frac{1}{2}
\cos(2\omega_c(t_1-t_2))\right]
\]

Let,

\[
\tau=t_1-t_2
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{R_X(\tau)=\frac{A^4}{4}
+\frac{A^4}{8}\cos(2\omega_c\tau)}
\]

Thus, the autocorrelation function depends only on the time difference \(\tau\).

### Step 4: Verify the WSS Conditions

The two conditions are verified as follows:

| WSS condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Mean is constant | \(E[X(t)]=A^2/2\) |
| Autocorrelation depends only on time difference | \(R_X(\tau)=\frac{A^4}{4}+\frac{A^4}{8}\cos(2\omega_c\tau)\) |

### Final Conclusion

Since both the conditions of Wide Sense Stationarity are satisfied,

\[
\boxed{X(t)\text{ is a WSS random process}}
\]

**Hence proved.**
