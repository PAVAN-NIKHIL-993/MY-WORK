DIY LC INDUCTANCE METER — BUILD GUIDE
====================================

PROJECT TYPE
------------
A low-voltage Arduino/LM393 experiment that estimates an unknown inductance
from the ringing frequency of a parallel LC tank.

This is an educational prototype, not a calibrated instrument. It measures
inductance only; it is not a full LCR meter. The schematic and sketch have not
been verified on physical hardware in this workspace. Do not use readings for
production, safety, or component qualification.

IMPORTANT CORRECTIONS AND LIMITS
--------------------------------
1. The Arduino excitation pin must be released to high impedance after the
   short pulse. Leaving it actively LOW clamps/damps the LC tank.
2. The sketch uses D8 (ATmega328P PB0), not D13. D13 has an onboard LED/load
   on common Uno/Nano boards. The sketch uses ATmega328P port registers for a
   fast release; it needs port-level adaptation for another MCU/board.
3. The source text labels D1 anode-to-tank and cathode-to-GND as a negative
   clamp. That polarity clamps positive excursions. For a negative clamp,
   connect D1 ANODE to GND and its banded CATHODE to the tank node.
4. A 1N4148 forward drop may still allow the tank below the LM393's negative
   input limit. The diode alone is not proof of safe input voltage. Check the
   exact comparator datasheet and scope the tank before connecting U1 pin 3.
   If the waveform exceeds common-mode or absolute-maximum limits, redesign
   the input clamp/bias network; do not rely on internal protection diodes.
5. LM393 output is open-collector: fit external R2 (10 kOhm) from pin 1 to
   +5 V. Do not omit it. Tie unused comparator inputs (pins 5 and 6) to GND;
   leave unused output pin 7 unconnected.
6. The source's “high accuracy”, 10 uH–100 mH guaranteed range, fixed 50 Ohm
   DCR cutoff, and <$2 cost claim are not verified. This sketch's pulseIn()
   measurement is software-timed and becomes coarse at higher frequencies.

IDEAL LC RELATION
-----------------
f0 = 1 / (2*pi*sqrt(Lx*C1))
Lx = 1 / (4*pi^2*f0^2*C1)

The real tank is damped and has coil resistance, parasitic capacitance,
comparator offset/delay, wiring effects, and measurement quantization. The
sketch treats a comparator HIGH pulse as approximately half a resonant cycle.
Averaging reduces some random jitter, not systematic errors.

With C1 = 1.0 uF, ideal frequencies are approximately:
  Lx = 100 mH  -> 503 Hz
  Lx =  10 mH  -> 1.59 kHz
  Lx =   1 mH  -> 5.03 kHz
  Lx = 100 uH  -> 15.9 kHz
  Lx =  10 uH  -> 50.3 kHz
Typical 16 MHz Uno-class pulseIn()/micros() resolution is on the order of
microseconds (commonly about 4 us). Treat this as a rough mH-range experiment,
not a reliable low-inductance meter. Better high-frequency readings require a
suitable front end and hardware capture/frequency-measurement method.

BILL OF MATERIALS
-----------------
Qty  Ref  Part
---  ---  ---------------------------------------------------------------
 1   MCU  Arduino Uno/Nano/Pro Mini, ATmega328P, 5 V logic only
 1   U1   LM393 dual comparator, DIP-8; check exact-part ratings
 1   C1   1.0 uF film capacitor, preferably 1-5% (measure if possible)
 1   C2   0.1 uF ceramic bypass capacitor, mounted close to U1 pins 8 and 4
 1   R1   1 kOhm, 1/4 W; series excitation resistor
 1   R2   10 kOhm, 1/4 W; LM393 open-collector pull-up
 1   D1   1N4148 switching diode; negative-clamp orientation below
 2   --   Test clips/socket pins for Lx
 1   Lx   Unknown inductor under test

A 3.3 V Pro Mini is not the target. Component prices and availability vary.

LM393 DIP-8 PINOUT (TOP VIEW; NOTCH/DOT AT TOP)
-----------------------------------------------
Pin  Name    Connection
---  ------  ------------------------------------------------------------
 1   OUT1    Arduino D5 and R2 (10 kOhm) to +5 V
 2   IN1-    GND reference (nominal zero threshold)
 3   IN1+    LC tank node, only after checking input voltage limits
 4   GND     Common GND
 5   IN2+    GND (defined input for unused comparator)
 6   IN2-    GND (defined input for unused comparator)
 7   OUT2    No connection (unused output)
 8   VCC     +5 V; bypass to pin 4 with C2

Verify the exact package and manufacturer's datasheet before wiring. The
classic LM393 is not rail-to-rail. Its input common-mode range and absolute
maximum input voltage must be observed.

CONNECTIONS
-----------
Arduino 5V --------------------------- +5 V rail
Arduino GND -------------------------- GND rail (common reference)

Arduino D8 (PB0) -- R1 1 kOhm -------- TANK NODE
                                          |-- Lx terminal 1
                                          |-- C1 terminal 1
                                          |-- D1 cathode (band)
                                          |-- U1 pin 3 (IN1+), after input check
Lx terminal 2 ------------------------ GND rail
C1 terminal 2 ------------------------ GND rail
D1 anode ----------------------------- GND rail

U1 pin 8 (VCC) ------------------------ +5 V rail
U1 pin 4 (GND) ------------------------ GND rail
C2 0.1 uF ----------------------------- directly across U1 pins 8 and 4
U1 pin 2 (IN1-) ----------------------- GND rail
U1 pin 1 (OUT1) ----------------------- Arduino D5
U1 pin 1 (OUT1) -- R2 10 kOhm -------- +5 V rail
U1 pins 5 (IN2+) and 6 (IN2-) -------- GND rail
U1 pin 7 (OUT2) ----------------------- leave unconnected

Lx and C1 are in parallel between the tank node and GND. All grounds must be
common. Place C2 next to U1. For D1, the stripe/band (cathode) goes to the tank
node; the unbanded anode goes to GND. D1 is a negative clamp, but it does not
guarantee that U1 pin 3 is within its limits.

ASSEMBLY STEPS
--------------
1. Use a low-voltage, current-limited 5 V supply and a 5 V ATmega328P board.
   Do not connect the prototype to mains or a high-energy circuit.
2. Connect Arduino 5V/GND to the breadboard rails and verify them with a meter.
3. Insert the LM393 DIP-8 across the breadboard center gap. Find pin 1 from
   the notch/dot and check the orientation.
4. Connect U1 pin 8 to +5 V and pin 4 to GND. Place C2 directly across these
   pins. Connect pin 2 to GND; connect unused pins 5 and 6 to GND; leave pin 7
   open.
5. Connect U1 pin 1 to Arduino D5 and fit R2 from pin 1 to +5 V.
6. Connect Arduino D8 through R1 to the tank node. Connect C1 and the test
   clips from the tank node to GND. Connect D1 with banded cathode to the tank
   node and anode to GND.
7. BEFORE connecting U1 pin 3, observe the tank waveform with an appropriate
   oscilloscope/probe. Check its full positive and negative swing against the
   exact LM393 input common-mode and absolute-maximum limits. Stop and redesign
   the input clamp/bias network if the waveform is outside those limits.
8. Only after that check, connect U1 pin 3 to the tank node. Verify the LM393
   output at pin 1/D5 and confirm the tank continues ringing when D8 is released.

SKETCH AND OPERATION
--------------------
Source file: lc_inductance_meter.ino
Diagram:     schematic.svg
Full project notes and calibration caveats: README.md

The sketch assumes the wiring above, a 5 V ATmega328P board, C1 = 1.0 uF, and
an external R2 pull-up. It uses PB0 registers because Arduino D8 maps to PB0
on these boards. Port the register operations before using another MCU.

1. Upload the sketch and open Serial Monitor at 9600 baud.
2. With no inductor connected, confirm that the sketch reports no reliable
   ringing instead of a plausible measurement.
3. Connect the unknown inductor across the two test clips.
4. Observe the frequency and estimated L. Repeat with a known reference part.

CALIBRATION
-----------
Use a known, low-tolerance inductor, keeping C1, clips, and layout unchanged.
The C_TANK_F setting should represent effective tank capacitance, including
relevant parasitics. A one-point scale correction is:

  C_new = C_old * (L_read / L_reference)

Example: if a known 10.00 mH part reads 9.40 mH with C_old = 1.000 uF, a
single-point adjustment is C_new about 0.940 uF. This compensates near that
reference and setup; it does not prove accuracy across a broad range.

TWO 5 mH INDUCTORS IN SERIES
----------------------------
For ideal uncoupled coils, Ltotal = L1 + L2 = 10 mH. If they are magnetically
coupled, Ltotal = L1 + L2 +/- 2M; the sign depends on winding orientation and
M is mutual inductance. Spacing/rotation can reduce coupling, but no fixed
distance or angle guarantees zero coupling. Measure the assembled pair. Series
DC resistance also adds and reduces Q/ring amplitude; there is no universal
50 Ohm pass/fail limit.

TROUBLESHOOTING
---------------
No pulses:
  Check common ground, U1 supply/orientation, R2 pull-up, D5 wiring, D8 release,
  coil continuity, and the tank waveform.

Ring stops immediately:
  Confirm D8 is not held LOW. Check R1, the parallel Lx/C1 connection, breadboard
  contacts, and coil loss. Shorten test leads where practical.

Readout unstable:
  Check C1 value/tolerance, coil DCR/Q, contact resistance, probe loading,
  comparator threshold/offset/noise, and signal amplitude.

Low-inductance values jump:
  The resonant frequency may exceed the useful timing range of pulseIn(). Use a
  suitable hardware capture method and a front end designed for that frequency.

SAFETY AND VERIFICATION
-----------------------
Use isolated, low-energy DC only. No mains use. Capacitors may retain charge;
coils can generate transients. Do not connect 5 V signals to 3.3 V-only inputs.
The exact tank waveform and LM393 input ratings must be verified before wiring
pin 3. No Arduino toolchain or target hardware was available for compilation or
bench verification in this workspace, so treat the sketch as a prototype.
